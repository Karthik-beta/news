# Major report pages

Draft, to be finalised in Phase 4. Use with `news-brand`.

## What the page answers
What the report's headline figures are, what changed since the previous edition, and what each main chapter says, in minutes instead of a 200-page PDF.

## Sources
The report itself (PDF and its data annex or database), the institution's press release and, if published, the official launch video.

## Rules specific to reports
- **Forecasts belong to the institution.** Present projections as published, credited in titles and subtitles ("The IMF projects…", "IMF projection"). Use tables for forecast grids; dashed lines only for the institution's own projections in charts.
- **Edition and date** in the subtitle or meta ("World Economic Outlook, October 2024").
- **Revisions since the last edition** are often the news: show them as the report does (percentage points), credited.
- **Chapter summaries are short** (two to four sentences each), in the report's own terms, with the chapter title in italics.
- **Quotes** only from the report or official statements, short and attributed.

## Typical layout
KeyFigures (headline projections) → Table of projections by region/economy as published → Bars of the largest revisions → Prose: one short section per main chapter → Note on what changed since the previous edition, as the report states it → Video of the launch if official.

## Headline patterns
- "The IMF projects global growth of 3.2% in both 2024 and 2025"
- "The World Bank cut its 2025 forecast for low-income countries"
