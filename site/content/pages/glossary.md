## About this glossary

This glossary explains the terms and labels that News uses on its pages. Pages link to a term instead of repeating its definition.

## A

**As reported.** The figure keeps the source's scale and precision, with nothing converted. A table marked "as reported" shows, for example, "US$ millions" and the source's own value.

## B

**Basis point.** One hundredth of a percentage point. News uses basis points for changes in interest rates, for example "cut by 25 basis points".

**bn, m, trn.** Billion, million and trillion, written without a space after the number, for example $65.6bn, €1.2trn and 420m.

## C

**Calculated by News from …** The label on a figure that is not in the source but is worked out by code from the source's own figures, such as a change from a year earlier or a share of a total. The formula is recorded, and code works it out only when the inputs can be compared like for like. See [methodology](/methodology).

## D

**Data as of.** The latest date on which any of the page's sources was retrieved.

## E

**Entity hub.** A page that lists everything News has published about one company, country, central bank or institution over time. Each quarter's page stays as published, and the hub keeps adding new ones.

## F

**Fiscal year.** A company's financial year, labelled the way the company labels it, for example "fiscal 2024". News does not restate a fiscal period as a calendar period. Microsoft's fiscal year, for example, runs from July to June.

## M

**MCP (Model Context Protocol).** A standard way for AI tools to query a service. News runs a read-only MCP server. See [data access](/data-access).

## P

**Percentage point.** The difference between two percentages. Pages write "points" for a change in a percentage, as in "up 2.1 points".

**Primary source.** The original public document or dataset a page is based on, such as a filing, an official statistic, an institutional report, a paper or a press release. The sources panel labels primary sources and linked news outlets differently.

## R

**Restated.** A company's or agency's revision of a figure it published earlier. A page shows a "restated" note when a prior period has been restated.

## S

**Sources panel.** The list on each page of every source the page uses, with its type (filing, dataset, report, paper, preprint, press release or news outlet), licence and required credit.

## T

**Tier 1 and Tier 2.** Tier 1 pages have a full visual dashboard and written commentary. Tier 2 pages show the main figures and charts with a short summary.

## U

**Updated on …** The note under a page's byline when the page has been corrected or revised. The sources panel gives a one-line reason. See [corrections](/corrections).

## W

**Withdrawn page.** A page that has been removed from the site's listings, public files and search. The record of it is kept.

## X

**XBRL.** A standard format that companies use to file financial data electronically. A figure's source record can name the XBRL tag it was read from.
