## What News is

News turns major company, economic and science reports into visual pages with every figure cited to its public source. It is open to people and to AI tools.

Reports such as quarterly results, IMF outlooks and central-bank decisions arrive as dense PDFs and formal prose. A News page shows the same public information as charts, dashboards, stat cards, timelines, embedded official video and source-linked images. Each figure links to the source it came from, so any reader can check it.

## Who runs it

News is a personal project run by one person, Karthik. It is free to read. It has no accounts, no ads and no paywall. It pays for no data, and it runs on free hosting tiers.

## How the pages are made

Every page says how it was made: built with Claude, an AI model, checked automatically, and run by Karthik. Figures are filled in from source data by code, and Claude does not type them. Read the [methodology](/methodology) for the full process and the rules.

## What it covers

News covers major company reports, economic releases, institutional reports, policy decisions, news stories and science. Pages are grouped into five categories: Tech, Business, Finance, Economy and Science. A page can belong to more than one category. Categories are stored as data, so they can change without changing any page's address.

Pages cover these types.

- Earnings, from company filings
- Macro releases, from statistics agencies and central banks
- Major reports, from institutions such as the IMF, the World Bank, the OECD and the BIS
- Policy decisions, from central banks and governments
- News stories, built from primary sources, with news outlets linked for context
- Science, from papers, preprints, agency releases and open datasets
- Explainers

Coverage is selective. News does not try to cover every story and does not report breaking news. It also does not publish market prices, forecasts of its own, ratings or investment opinions.

Regions followed are North America, Europe, India, China, other parts of Asia and Australia, plus global institutions. Pages are in English.

## Who it is for

News is for readers who follow technology, business, finance, the economy and science, and who want the substance of a report in a few minutes, with the numbers visible and the source one tap away. AI assistants and other tools can also read and cite the pages. See [data access](/data-access).
